# Dan Abramov — Open Social

**URL:** https://overreacted.io/open-social/

**Date accessed:** 2026-09-16

## What It Covers

A detailed article explaining the philosophy of "Open Social" — the idea that social data should work like the open web. Written by Dan Abramov (creator of Redux, formerly at React team and Bluesky).

## Key Information Extracted

### The Core Thesis
- "What open source did for code, open social does for data"
- We are at the same juncture with social apps as we were with open source 35 years ago
- AT Protocol is "the most convincing take on it so far"

### The Problem: Closed Social
- Pre-social web: you owned your website at your domain, hosting was a commodity
- Social apps: your data lives in someone else's database. You're a row in their table.
- You can't walk away without losing your audience
- "If you can't leave without losing something important, the platform has no incentives to respect you"

### The Solution: Open Social
- Your data lives on a server you choose
- Apps aggregate over your data, not own it
- Products are temporary, your data is permanent
- You can move between apps without starting from scratch
- "The web we've created remains after the products we used to create it are gone"

### How It Works (Analogy)
- **Before social** — personal websites, HTML, links, hosting independence
- **Closed social** — one database per app, aggregation across users, but data is trapped
- **Open social** — user-owned data, apps as aggregators, data outlives products

### Key Concept: Portability
- Account portability is the critical feature
- Links between data should be logical (between users), not physical (between servers)
- Apps should compete on quality, not on lock-in

### Memorable Quote
- "People might not care about 'federation' but they do notice when they log into a competing product and their data is already there, and their reach is intact"
- "People do understand when they're being fucked with"

### Timeline Prediction
- "Like with open source, open social is a compounding effort"
- "Every mildly successful open social app lifts all open social apps"
- "At some point, open is bound to win"
- "I just hope it doesn't take thirty-five years"
